Formulas

$$ r=\frac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2\sum (y_i-\bar y)^2}} $$Pearson correlation
$$ t=\frac{r\sqrt{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}},\quad df=n-2 $$test of H₀: ρ = 0
$$ \hat\beta_1=\frac{S_{xy}}{S_{xx}}=r\frac{s_y}{s_x} $$OLS slope
$$ \hat\beta_0=\bar y-\hat\beta_1\bar x $$OLS intercept
$$ \hat{\boldsymbol\beta}=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y $$OLS in matrix form (any number of predictors)
$$ t_j=\frac{\hat\beta_j}{SE(\hat\beta_j)} $$coefficient t-test, df = n − p − 1
$$ TSS=ESS+RSS,\quad R^2=1-\frac{RSS}{TSS} $$variation decomposition
$$ \bar R^2=1-(1-R^2)\frac{n-1}{n-p-1} $$adjusted R² (p = number of predictors)
$$ s=\sqrt{\frac{RSS}{n-p-1}} $$residual standard error
$$ F=\frac{(RSS_r-RSS_f)/q}{RSS_f/(n-p-1)} $$nested models: restricted vs full, q dropped terms
$$ \hat y_0\pm t\,s\sqrt{\tfrac1n+\tfrac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}} $$confidence interval for the mean
$$ \hat y_0\pm t\,s\sqrt{1+\tfrac1n+\tfrac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{S_{xx}}} $$prediction interval for one case
$$ AIC=2k-2\ln L,\quad BIC=k\ln n-2\ln L $$lower is better
$$ \text{simple regression: } R^2=r^2,\;F=t^2 $$handy checks
$$ \min_{\beta}\sum_i \rho\!\left(\frac{y_i-x_i^\top\beta}{s}\right) $$M-estimation (OLS is ρ(u) = u²)
$$ w_{\text{Huber}}=\min\!\left(1,\tfrac{k}{|u|}\right),\quad w_{\text{bisq}}=\left(1-\tfrac{u^2}{c^2}\right)^2\ (|u|<c) $$k = 1.345, c = 4.685; bisquare weight is 0 beyond c
R.3

Hypothesis tests at a glance

One rule for all of them: p-value < α (0.05) → reject H₀.

TestH₀Where in RIf p < 0.05…
correlation t-testρ = 0cor.test(x, y)the correlation is significant
coefficient t-testβⱼ = 0summary(fit) → Pr(>|t|)Xⱼ matters, given the other predictors
overall F-testall slopes = 0summary(fit) → last linethe model explains something
nested-model F-testthe smaller model is as goodanova(small, big)the extra terms are needed → keep the bigger model
Shapiro–Wilkresiduals are normalshapiro.test(fit$resid)residuals are not normal
Harvey–Collierthe relationship is linearharvtest(fit, order.by = ~ x)the relationship is not linear
Goldfeld–Quandtconstant error variance (homoscedasticity)gqtest(fit, order.by = ~ x)heteroscedasticity: the variance changes

Robust fits (summary(rlm(...))) give no p-values: use |t value| > 2 as a rough 5% rule.

Classic traps
  • A large p-value does not prove H₀ — it only means "not enough evidence against it".
  • Significant ≠ important. With huge n, tiny effects get ***.
  • R² without an intercept is not comparable with R² with one.
  • Correlation ≠ causation.

Interpretation templates

correlation

"There is a [strong/moderate/weak] [positive/negative] linear relationship between X and Y (r = __). It is [significant/not significant] at 5% (t(__) = __, p = __)."

slope

"For each one-unit increase in X, Y is expected to change by β̂ units, holding the other predictors constant."

intercept

"The expected Y when all predictors are 0" — and say whether X = 0 makes sense in the data.

R²

"__% of the variation in Y is explained by the model; the rest is due to factors not included."

residual SE

"Predictions are typically off by about __ units of Y."

intervals

"We are 95% confident that the mean Y for X = x₀ lies in [CI]; a single new observation will lie in [PI]."

R.5

Regression workflow checklist

  1. Load data; check dim, summary, NAs (md.pattern).
  2. Handle missing values (delete if few; otherwise mice, not mean imputation).
  3. Plot Y against each X; compute the correlation matrix (Pearson and Spearman).
  4. Fit lm; read signs, sizes, p-values, R², adjusted R², F.
  5. Compare candidate models with anova (nested) or AIC/BIC.
  6. Check residuals: vs fitted, Q-Q, histogram, shapiro.test, plot(fit); linearity (harvtest) and constant variance (gqtest).
  7. Outliers? If ≤ 5% they may be removed (with a reason); otherwise fit robust estimators (rlm, lqs) and keep the one with the lowest residual standard error.
  8. Only then interpret and predict — inside the range of the data.
